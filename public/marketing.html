Commercial proof before scale

Find three businesses.
Prove one useful outcome.

Start with founder-led recruitment. Use a small Google Search test only once a real call reliably becomes a callback enquiry.

Proposed budget, not approved spend: £150 initial Google Ads media test. An optional second £150 requires a review. Nothing has been launched or purchased. Hosting, voice usage and founder time are separate costs.

1. The customer and the promise

Focus on owner-operated plumbing businesses with roughly 1–5 people, an existing business number, and calls missed while working. Start in southwest London for manageable onboarding and feedback. This is a targeting hypothesis, not a claim about market size.

The offer: “Keep your number. Capture missed plumbing enquiries. Get the details you need to call back.” Offer three manually supported pilot places. A 14-day pilot begins only after a test call and forwarding verification pass. Confirm the voice usage allowance and any post-pilot charge before advertising “free”.

Do not promise recovered revenue, instant emergency attendance or automatic booking. A captured enquiry is not a won job. Ask prospects how many calls they miss, what happens today, who returns calls, and whether they would trust an AI receptionist with their business name.

2. Earn the first evidence without ads

  1. Days 1–3: Barney and David each identify five businesses through existing contacts and ask for a 15-minute conversation. Use permission-based introductions; no bulk outreach is scheduled.
  2. Days 4–7: show the PoC to five interested owners. Have each describe a real missed-call scenario. Record objections in their own words: voice quality, forwarding, pricing, trust, or callback workload.
  3. Next: recruit up to three pilots, configure each manually and prove the call path. Track owner-confirmed useful enquiries and whether callbacks happened.
  4. Partner test: approach two web designers or local marketing freelancers who already serve trades. Ask for feedback and introductions; do not buy a referral programme before conversion is demonstrated.

These numbers are activity targets. A small pilot cannot establish product-market fit or reliable acquisition economics. It can uncover the reasons owners will or will not use the service.

3. Readiness gate before any Google spend

  • Real UK call → voice receptionist → saved enquiry → owner notification verified.
  • At least one owner has used it and confirmed that the enquiry was actionable.
  • A public, mobile-friendly marketing page with one clear “Book a short demo” action, genuine contact details and an accurate privacy notice. The private PoC inbox is not an ad destination.
  • Lead form and confirmation tested; analytics and consent behaviour verified. Demo events excluded from acquisition reporting.
  • Someone is available to respond to enquiries the same working day.

5. Measure commercial intent

Primary conversion: a valid demo request from a plumbing business owner or decision-maker. Secondary events: demo started and demo completed. Track pilot activated and paying customer separately. Page views and clicks on “Try demo” are not sales leads.

Record source, campaign, search term where available, spend, qualified demos, pilots and paid customers. Use UTMs. In PostHog/Google Ads, exclude internal testing and never send phone numbers, names, postcodes, call transcripts or customer enquiry text as event properties. PostHog and Google Ads tracking are not connected in this PoC.

6. Stop and continuation rules

  • Pause immediately if tracking, the form or the call workflow fails.
  • At £50 spend, review relevance. If clearly wrong traffic dominates, pause and fix targeting before spending more.
  • At £100 with no qualified demo, pause for a landing-page and offer review. Do not automatically increase the budget.
  • At £150 stop. Release the optional next £150 only after reviewing actual queries and at least one qualified conversation, plus evidence that onboarding can work.
  • If impressions are low but relevant, review search volume before loosening targeting. No-spend/low-volume is not proof that customers do not want the product.

Set a sustainable acquisition target only after pricing and costs are known: monthly contribution = subscription revenue − voice/number costs − variable support and infrastructure. A proposed initial acquisition ceiling is three months of that contribution. Compare this with actual cost per paying customer, not cost per click.

7. Who does what

Barney: product readiness, supplier costs and onboarding.
David: prospect conversations, objections and pilot feedback, subject to his agreement.
Joint weekly review: useful enquiries, owner callbacks, quality failures, usage cost and willingness to pay.

Do not buy extra channels, premium analytics or a CRM for the initial test. The immediate decision is whether a small set of plumbing businesses finds the service useful enough to keep.
